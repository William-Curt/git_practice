About this dataset

What the log shows. A ±10 V triangle sweep (1.12 V/s, 17.9 s per half-sweep) on a small probe in a tenuous plasma whose density stepped through several levels over 24 minutes. The current channel carries an instrument offset of about 148 nA plus a ≈1.7 nA/V leakage ramp (≈600 MΩ); both are visible directly in the last seven sweeps (75–81), where the plasma is off, and they are removed by the default “plasma-off fit” baseline.

What that means for the numbers. After the baseline, plasma sweeps show a floating potential of −2.5 to −4.8 V, an ion current of only 10–75 nA at −9.5 V that is still growing with |V| (no flat ion saturation), and an electron current that keeps rising all the way to +10 V. No sweep reaches a knee, so the plasma potential lies above +10 V (sheath theory puts it near 11–13 V) and electron saturation is never seen: ne is a lower bound and Te is an effective slope temperature over the fit window. The electron branch is not a single exponential: the local slope temperature rises from about 1 eV just above Vf to about 6 eV near +8 V (switch the lower panel to “Local Te(V)” to see it). Up- and down-sweeps are offset by 0.4–1 V of hysteresis. Two episodes (sweeps 9–10 and 59–60) are over-range transients where the ADC wrapped from +2.9 µA to −2.1 µA; those samples are excluded from every fit and drawn in grey. With Debye lengths of several millimetres the probe is in the orbital-motion-limited regime, so the planar saturation-current formulas are approximations.

How each number is computed

  1. Half-sweeps are cut wherever the DAC ramp reverses direction (0 ↔ 65535); each is 1457 samples, 17.9 s.
  2. Over-range screening drops samples where I2 is below −50 nA or jumps by more than 1 µA between neighbours (the ADC wrap-around), before anything else.
  3. Baseline: a straight line fitted to the plasma-off sweeps (default 75–80) is subtracted; a constant or no baseline are also available.
  4. Smoothing is a centred moving average over the chosen window; dI/dV is the least-squares slope over the same window. The residual scatter gives the noise σI.
  5. Signal gate: a sweep needs a current span above 10 σI and a slope above 5 σb to be analysed; otherwise it is reported as “no plasma signal”. Partial sweeps (not reaching ±8 V) are shown but not analysed.
  6. Ion-saturation fit: a straight line through the most negative part of the sweep (the chosen fraction). Its extrapolation Iion(V) is subtracted to give the electron current Ie. Iis is the mean current over the lowest 1 V; the fit extrapolated to Vf is shown as a secondary value.
  1. Vf (floating potential): where the smoothed current crosses zero, with an uncertainty σI/(dI/dV). Multiple crossings are resolved by a local line fit and flagged.
  2. Vp (plasma potential): the maximum of dI/dV, accepted only when it lies at least 1 V inside the sweep and the slope falls to 70 % of the peak beyond it. Otherwise the knee is beyond +10 V and the sheath estimate Vf + Te ln(√(mi/2πme)/0.61) is shown instead.
  3. Te: slope of ln Ie vs V over the transition window, Te = 1/slope in eV, with R². The window's lower and upper halves are fitted separately; a ratio above 1.3 flags a non-exponential branch.
  4. Ies: electron current at Vp, or the peak electron current of the sweep (normally at +10 V) as a lower bound when no knee is found.
  5. ne = Ies / (e A √(eTe/2πme)); ni = |Iis| / (0.61 e A √(eTe/mi)); λD = √(ε₀Te/nee). Both densities scale as 1/A, so set the real collecting area.
  6. Hysteresis: each up-sweep is paired with the down-sweep that follows it; the value is the bias shift that best aligns the down-sweep onto the up-sweep over −5 … +9 V (positive: the down-sweep reaches each current at a higher bias).

Channels: I2 is the main current channel (0.15 nA steps); I1 carries the same current with coarser 0.9 nA steps and reads NaN above ≈1.7 µA, where it is dropped. V1 in the log is an attenuated monitor of the bias (V1 ≈ −0.166·V2 + 0.17) and is not plotted. Keyboard: ← → step sweeps, space plays.
